Adaptive Dark Mode

Presents light websites as dark, the way Safari 27.3 does: it adapts colors by role, keeps hues, aims for 4.5:1 text contrast, and leaves photos, video and canvas alone.

Your sites

Sites you turned on with “Try” or the toolbar switch, and sites you turned off.

Quirks

Some sites need special handling, as in Safari: turn the mode off for a site, force the color-preserving inversion on specific elements, or keep elements exactly as authored. Selectors are CSS selectors.

How it works

  1. Gates. It runs only while the system is dark, on sites you allowed, unless the site declares color-scheme: light dark or light only, or already renders dark.
  2. Rule-level transform. Every author rule that sets a color gets a mirrored override with the same selector, in the same media/layer/supports context, placed after the page’s sheets. Light surfaces become dark with a contrast-preserving luminance flip that keeps hue. Already-dark surfaces stay dark. Vivid colors keep their meaning.
  3. Backdrop pass. For text in view it finds what actually paints behind it, the same idea as WebKit’s two-phase backdrop paint, and pushes it to the contrast target. Text over photos keeps the author’s colors.
  4. Images. Simple black-and-white logos and dark monochrome SVG icons are inverted while keeping their hue. Photos, video and canvas are left alone.

Limits compared with Safari: an extension can’t hide its changes from page scripts, so computed styles show the adapted colors. Image sprites are classified as a whole.
